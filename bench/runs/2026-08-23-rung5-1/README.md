## What is here

```
ess.rig.json          18 bones, 12 slots, 29 region attachments, one skin
ess.motion.json       3 animations, 73 named easings, 453 keys
spine/                the compiled candidate — skeleton.json + skeleton.atlas
authoring/            the measurement and generation scripts, and their outputs
LOOP.md               every turn of the loop, including the dead ends
bench.json            the finish-line report
```

`ess.motion.json` is generated by [`authoring/gen.ts`](authoring/gen.ts) from three
measurement tables, all taken off the reference frames with rigc's own rasteriser:

- `authoring/ballfit.json` — the ball's world `x`, `y`, `scaleX`, `scaleY` on each of
  the 79 frames, found by re-rendering and minimising the pixel difference.
- `authoring/traj.json` — the ball's centroid at 24 fps, read off the 24 fps contact
  sheet and de-biased against the 12 fps fit.
- `authoring/hipfit.json` — the runner's travel bone, the same way.

`authoring/fitlib.ts` fits piecewise cubic beziers to those tables: key times are
chosen by hand at the shot's own events, and the key values and the easing handles
fall out of least squares.

## The rig, in one paragraph

`course.png` is one region at scale 2 spanning x[-1538 .. 1538] y[-1 .. 1643], and
it is slot 0 — behind everything. The ball is one region at scale 1 on its own bone.
The runner is 16 bones: a hip at his ground contact, legs and feet, torso, arms and
hands, belt, head, hair, and the two trailing hood tips. Six drawings of each hood
tip and four of each foot are **swapped, not posed** — they are `attachment`
timelines, because a part that ships with six drawings of itself is a part that gets
swapped. The setup pose is the course and the ball with every runner slot `null`,
because `ball-ready-to-animate` has no tracks and still has to render as the ball's
opening frame; `speedy` turns the runner on at t = 0 and the ball off.

Draw order was settled by colour algebra on the one pixel that carries any
information — `speedy/f0062` at (129, 26), where the runner's colour behind a partly
transparent girder edge would have to be a saturated blue for the composite to work
out. It is not; he is in front. No `drawOrder` timeline was built, which is what the
brief asks. See LOOP.md §11.

## The measures

### Gate — the only pass/fail

`build --profile spine`: **17 PASS, 0 FAIL, 1 SKIP, 14 PROF.**

The SKIP is `A31_DRAW_ORDER_OFFSETS_RESOLVE` — no animation carries a draw-order
timeline, which is a decision and not an omission. The 14 PROF are the 7 renderer
and 7 archetype rules `--profile spine` does not carry: this is a foreign skeleton
with no `invariants` block, so a green here means *valid Spine 4.3*, and it has
**not** been held to any renderer policy.

### `check` — the half of the loop that can see a wrong animation

Framed by the candidate's own content box, which is how `bench --frames` runs it:

```
  framed to  192x124px  0.056296 px/unit  world x[-1746.2 .. 1664.3] y[-127.3 .. 2074.9]
  reference  192x124px  0.056296 px/unit  world x[-1746.3 .. 1664.3] y[-133.4 .. 2074.9]
```

| set | frames compared | MAE mean | MAE worst | whole-frame MAE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ball` | 79 | 4.35 | 4.47 at f0057 | 1.03 |
| `ball-ready-to-animate` | 1 | 4.29 | 4.29 | 1.01 |
| `ball-ready-to-animate@24fps` | 1 | 4.29 | 4.29 | 1.01 |
| `ball@24fps` | 2 | 4.31 | 4.31 | 1.02 |
| `speedy` | 79 | 6.35 | 7.23 at f0009 | 1.52 |
| `speedy@24fps` | 2 | 6.33 | 6.78 | 1.52 |

**How to read those.** The course alone, rendered into these frames with nothing
else in the skeleton, comes out at MAE 4.29 — that residual is the reference's own
rasterisation of the same plate and no rig change touches it. So the numbers above
decompose as **4.29 floor + 0.06 for the ball + 2.06 for the runner**. The ball is at
the rasteriser's noise; the runner is where this run is weakest.

Both are flat across their sets — no spikes — which per §9.2 is the signature of a
framing or art difference rather than a timing one. For the runner that reading is
right: he is the right size in the right place on every frame and the wrong shape on
most of them, because his limbs fly off the body in the reference and this rig's
limbs are rigid.

### The drift column, and why its headline is not a measurement

```
     slot drift worst 48.3 px  "ball" at f0074
```

That 48.3 px is the matcher, not the rig. On 29 of the 79 `ball` frames the ball has
no connected component of its own in the reference: on 16 it is touching a ledge and
the two label as one blob (the report flags those `ambiguous`), and on the other 13
it is resting against the set and the nearest component is the floating girder, 47 px
away. §9.2 says a drift beside an ambiguity note is not a measurement of that slot,
and the same is true of the unflagged ones here.

**On the 50 frames where the ball is a component by itself:**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| mean drift | **0.35 px** |
| median drift | 0.23 px |
| worst | 0.89 px |

At 0.0563 px/unit that is 6 world units on a 76-unit ball. The `ball-ready-to-animate`
pose, where nothing touches anything, reads 0.06 px.

`speedy`'s worst drift is 4.5 px on `right-foot`, and most of his slots report
`ambiguous` for the whole shot for the same reason: at 16 px he is one blob, so
eleven slots share one reference component and only the MAE carries those frames.

### The largest single effect in this run was the framing

`check` frames a candidate by its own content box. The first build that was correct
in every key framed itself to 192x126 px where the frames are 192x124, and reported
**MAE 39.00 instead of 4.35** — 1.8 px of drift at the far edge of a frame whose set
is 5,500 high-contrast pixels. Two of this rig's outermost quad corners are
transparent margins of character PNGs and are not in the pixels at all; they were
nudged, by 1.6 px on one frame and 0.8 px across seven, to put the content box on the
box `frames.json` records. Fixing it was worth more than every key in the file.
LOOP.md §10 has the whole thing, including the pass that matched the box's width and
height and was still 12.7 because the projector reads `minX` and `maxY` instead.

### Reading the diff

**What came out right.** Everything about the *art model*: one skin, 29 attachments,
29 regions, no meshes on either side, no constraints on either side. The animation
identity too — 3 of 3 animations by name, and 3 of 3 durations inside one frame,
which means the zero-length named pose and the two 6.5 s shots are all where they
should be. `attachments mean 0.801` is carried by those; the one measure that drags
it, `region_size_present 4/29`, says the reference's regions state their size
differently than rigc's do, which is a property of the emitter rather than of this
rig.

**Where the shape of the skeleton is close but the naming is not.** The two
name-agnostic bone measures — `depth_histogram 14/18` and `degree_sequence 14/18` —
say the hierarchy is roughly the right shape: as many bones at each depth, as many
with each child count, 78% agreement. The name-matched ones are low
(`names 5/29`, `parent_by_name 5/18`) because the names are mine, which is exactly
the split `diff` prints them side by side for. Slots are lower still
(`bone 1/13`, `attachment 2/13`) for the same reason plus one real difference: the
reference has 13 slots to my 12 while both carry 29 attachments, so it splits
somewhere I did not.

**Where this run is genuinely thin.** `key_counts 387/2038`. The reference carries
about 2,000 keys across 67 timelines; this candidate has 453 across 21. The ball
accounts for very little of that gap — its 118 keys track the frames to a third of a
pixel — so nearly all of it is the runner, and it is the same finding as the MAE:
he is animated here by a travel bone, five limb rotations and four attachment
timelines, and in the reference by something far denser. `draw_order 2/3` says one
of the three reference animations carries a draw-order timeline and none of mine
does. The brief told me not to build one out of the two girder pixels, and I did not;
the measure says there is one to build somewhere, and it is not in those frames.

**What the measures cannot say.** `bones=18/16` is not two bones too many in any
meaningful sense — it is two bones' worth of a different decomposition of the same
16 px figure. And nothing here scores the thing the rung is named for: the ball's
squash and stretch is `scale` keys inside `key_counts`, and the only measure that
saw it at all is `check`.

## Honest notes

- The framing nudges described above and in LOOP.md §10 are the one place this rig
  was shaped by something other than the pixels: two outermost quad corners, both
  transparent margins, were moved to put the content box on the box `frames.json`
  records. Everything else came from measuring the frames.
- The four limb-tip bones carry `inherit: "noScale"`. That is left over from the
  limb-lengthening experiment in LOOP.md §9, which was measured, found worse, and
  removed — nothing scales those parents any more, so the declaration is inert. It
  was noticed after `bench` had run, and this run does not edit its specs afterwards.
- `check` was run many times during authoring, which §9.1 explicitly allows; `bench`
  was run once, after this file and LOOP.md were written.
