    ── chains ──
    Cut from the CANDIDATE's own bone tree at every branch point: a chain runs from a root or a fork down to the
    next fork, a single-bone chain that is itself a fork folds into its parent, and each is named after the first
    bone in it that carries a slot. The reference is still nothing but pixels — this is your figure decomposed,
    not the reference’s, which is what keeps it inside the ladder’s honesty rule.
  
    MAE share divides the difference over the REFERENCE's own drawn pixels — the denominator from the MAE
    line above, which nothing you draw can grow — and splits it by giving each of those pixels to the chain
    whose ink is NEAREST it. So the shares are a partition and add to the whole, and no chain can look
    better by drawing more: growing its ink only pulls more of the reference’s pixels, and their error,
    into it. `MAE in it` is the same error per pixel it took, and it is the column that separates a chain
    that is WRONG from one that is merely large — a head and its features cover a lot of a figure and can
    carry a third of the error at a below-average figure per pixel.
  
    ⚠️ Two things the split cannot do, both of which show rather than hide. Reference ink further from your
    ink than the part’s own size is left `(unattributed)` instead of blamed on a neighbour, so a part that
    has left its place stops being charged to whatever is next to it. And a chain that draws NOTHING seeds
    nothing and reads 0 % — which is why the slots column is beside the share: 0 % on 0 slots drawn is the
    loudest row here, not the quietest.

    MAE is the mean absolute RGB difference over the pixels either side covers, so it is
    read against 255 and not against a threshold: there is no pass mark here any more than
    there is one in `diff`. The figure under it divides the same difference by the pixels the
    REFERENCE drew — a denominator you cannot grow, which is what makes it the one to author
    against; it is not bounded by 255. Read the framing line first: it is upstream of every number
    below, and a residual much wider than a pixel moves all of them at once.
    The slots column is how many of the drawn slots could be attributed at all. A drift
    marked `tmpl` was correlated against the slot’s own pixels because the reference
    merged it into a neighbour; the number beside it is how much better that match was
    than its best rival, and a slot that matched nothing at all is left out of the count.
    A `sheet` line is the frames a set does not commit as files: the candidate sampled at the
    set's own rate against the tiles of its contact.png, in the same box the frame table used.
    Read it as a series — flat is framing or art, a spike is timing at that moment — and note
    that it is MAE only: the change columns below are pixel counts at frame scale.
    `Δpx` and `ref Δ` are how many pixels each side moved since ITS OWN previous frame —
    not against each other. They are the only columns that can see a held pose that is
    not held, or a one-frame event that never fired: both are small in every frame and
    wrong only in the relation between two, which is where the MAE cannot look.
