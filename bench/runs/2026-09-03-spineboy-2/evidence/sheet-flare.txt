The muzzle flare's window, off the 30 fps contact sheets
========================================================

WHY THE SHEET AND NOT THE FRAMES
--------------------------------
An attachment timeline is stepped, and AUTHORING §4.5's 🚨 says a stepped key is
either on the sample that was meant to see it or a whole frame out. The 12 fps set
brackets this flare's end between 0.3333 s and 0.4167 s — a whole twelfth of
uncertainty on a three-frame event. §9.3's ⭐ names the sheet as the only place that
closes it: "on a set rendered at a higher rate than the frames on disk, every sampled
frame is compared, so the samples between two committed ones are measured there and
nowhere else."

THE LAYOUT, AND ITS CONTROL
---------------------------
The tile geometry is the one the BRIEF states — "128 px long side, 1 px rules, 8
columns, row major" — so `bench/render_reference.ts`, which is on the forbidden list,
was not opened. The control is arithmetic and it is enforced rather than described:
`tools/sheet.ts` reconstructs each sheet's own pixel size from that geometry and the
set's frame count, and REFUSES to read a sheet whose size does not match.

  set            tiles   tile size   columns x rows   reconstructed   file
  ------------   -----   ---------   --------------   -------------   ----
  death@30fps      149   128 x 122        8 x 19       1033 x 2338    exact
  hit@30fps         11   128 x 122        8 x  2       1033 x  247    exact
  idle@30fps        51   128 x 122        8 x  7       1033 x  862    exact
  jump@30fps        41   128 x 122        8 x  6       1033 x  739    exact
  run@30fps         21   128 x 122        8 x  3       1033 x  370    exact
  shoot@30fps       13   128 x 122        8 x  2       1033 x  247    exact
  walk@30fps        31   128 x 122        8 x  4       1033 x  493    exact

`aim@30fps` ships one frame and therefore no sheet, which is one of the three reasons
G7 reads SKIP.

THE FLARE, PER TILE
-------------------
The predicate is the brief's own, recomputed here rather than taken: a drawn pixel with
r > 200, b > 140 and g < min(r, b) − 30, over the mask at 8/255.

  shoot@30fps   t5=20  t6=16  t7=191  t8=190  t9=383  t10=72  t11=81
  every other set: zero on every tile

⇒ tiles 5..11, brightest at tile 9. That reproduces the brief's claim exactly ("on the
30 fps sheet it is on tiles 5 through 11, brightest at tile 9"), and it is the fifth
independent reading of it.

WHAT WAS AUTHORED FROM IT
-------------------------
  show at  5/30 = 0.166667 s   (minus one 1e-6 grid step, §4.5)
  hide at 12/30 = 0.400000 s   (minus one 1e-6 grid step)

The hide key at 0.4 rather than at the 12 fps f5 time of 0.4167 is the whole point: at
0.4167 the 30 fps samples 12 (0.4000) would still see the flare, and tile 12 is measured
CLEAR. The brief's sentence "the flare is gone by 0.400 s" is the same fact.

⚠️ WHAT THIS DOES NOT DECIDE
----------------------------
Which of the five numbered plates is on which tile. The brief says the frames "cannot
tell you how they are divided between parts or which flare is on which frame", and the
sheet does not change that: a tile is the frame at 1/3 scale, so a 133 x 79 plate is
44 x 26 tile pixels and the five plates are not separable there. This run authors ONE
plate for the whole window, chosen on the full-resolution 12 fps frames by
`tools/skins.ts`'s sweep, and the sweep's own margin is recorded beside it. A
five-key sequence swapping plate per frame is a shape the frames do not support and was
not authored.
