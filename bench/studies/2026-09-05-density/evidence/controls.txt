# How every figure in this study was produced

One command. The harness writes its own art, its own three depth sheets and its
own rig and motion specs into the work directory, so nothing here depends on a
corpus, on private art, or on anything outside this repository.

    bun bench/studies/2026-09-05-density/tools/densprobe.ts <workdir>

    # optional: the cost table at another angle. The fold ladder ignores it,
    # since the fold ladder's whole job is to find the angle.
    bun bench/studies/2026-09-05-density/tools/densprobe.ts <workdir> --degrees=20

⚠️ <workdir> is required and must sit OUTSIDE this repository. The harness had a
default of `render/` — git-ignored, so invisible — and the rig and motion specs
it left there were swept up by the selftest's own walk over every motion spec in
the tree, which reported 66 where the repository holds 38. Nothing asserted that
count, so nothing went red; a harness that writes into the tree it measures is
the same hazard as running an indexer while something else is still writing.

Its four tables are `cost.txt`, `keycost.txt`, `depth.txt` and `fold.txt` in
this directory, in that order and unedited.

## What is fixed, and what is swept

  part window        400 x 400 px, an ellipse of radius 0.46 x window,
                     checkerboarded so a reversed triangle is visible in a render
  zScale             60 world units, stated in the rig spec like a radius
  depth sheets       dome    z = Z sqrt(1 - r^2)          — vertical at the rim
                     cosine  z = Z (1 + cos(pi r)) / 2    — bounded slope everywhere
                     flat    one level, 128               — the no-gradient control
  yaw                one `transform` key, kind `yaw`, `depth: true`, at t = 1s
  cost table angle   12 degrees, which every rung admits
  triangle budget    invariants.meshTriangles = 1,000,000, so the budget is never
                     the thing being measured
  profile            `spine`, the CLI default
  swept              grid cols = rows in {5, 9, 17, 33, 65, 97, 129, 181}
                     contour tolerance in {8, 4, 2, 1, 0.5, 0.25}, maxVertices 100000

## Which numbers are measurements and which are predictions

Measured: every figure in `cost.txt`, `keycost.txt` and `depth.txt`, and the
three "last admitted" columns of `fold.txt`. The z values are recovered from the emitted
`skeleton.json` — bind x out of the weighted run, offset out of the deform key —
rather than read back off the report, and the triangle areas likewise.

Predicted: the two "predicted" columns of `fold.txt`, from the closed forms in
`predictedFoldDegrees` and `predictedCosineFoldDegrees`. They take no measured
input; they are the absolute reference the ladder is read against.

## Timings

Wall-clock on one machine (Apple silicon, bun 1.3.11), single run, no repeats
and no warm-up discarded. ⚠️ They are quoted for their SHAPE — the ratio between
rungs — and not as a benchmark.

⭐ The reading that says so out loud is in the table itself: `contour-8`, at
SIXTEEN vertices, compiles in 178.85 ms — 3.4x the 32,761-vertex lattice — and
it does that because it is the first compile the process runs and pays for
loading everything under it. So a rung's absolute millisecond is not a figure;
the slope across the eight lattice rungs is. No conclusion here rests on a
difference under a factor of two.

The byte counts and the fold ladder carry no such caveat. Both reproduce
exactly across runs, which is what `A18_DETERMINISTIC_EMIT` exists to make true.
