# reader probe — offsets

invocation: bun bench/studies/2026-09-06-readers/tools/readerprobe.ts --experiment=offsets
seed 20260906, 40,000 sweep samples, spine-core 4.3.13

A slider always passes `Slider.offsets`, which is all zeros. A `TransformConstraint` passes its own,
and the same six readers serve both — so every figure in this study is stated FOR ZERO OFFSETS, and
this is what the other case costs.

The array is indexed `[ROTATION, X, Y, SCALEX, SCALEY, SHEARY]`.

     worldX`, so the offset is applied in the BONE's frame and comes out scaled and rotated.

## …and what it does to the BOUNDS, not just the reading

| offsets[ROTATION] / [SCALEX] | `rotate` world span | `scaleX` world floor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | [0.000000, 359.999995] | 0 |
| 100 | [0.000004, 359.950009] | 100 |
| -100 | [0.000004, 359.999999] | -100 |
| 400 | [220.000005, 579.999995] | 400 |
| -400 | [-219.999995, 139.999995] | -400 |

⇒ **the two scale floors and the `shearY` window depend on the offsets being zero**: the floor IS the
   offset, and the `shearY` window slides by it.

⇒ **`rotate` world is the one that does not — until the offset leaves the circle.** The wrap runs
   AFTER the offset is added, so any offset in [−360, 360] is renormalised and the set is `[0, 360)`
   again, moved only in WHICH bone angle reads what. At ±400 there is nothing left to wrap and the
   span moves bodily — measured above. A slider can never get there (`Slider.offsets` is zero), so
   this row is for the reader who reaches these classes through a `TransformConstraint`.
