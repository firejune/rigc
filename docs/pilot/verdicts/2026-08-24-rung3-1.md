**Below ceiling — the finding:** `animations.timeline_kinds` **4/8** (baselines
8/8). The reference keys `translatex`/`translatey` as separate timelines; the
pilot keyed the paired `translate` form throughout. This is a failure the guide
already answers — [`AUTHORING.md`](../../AUTHORING.md) §4.4 ("Single-axis
timelines are not sugar … use the single-axis form … when the two axes need
different key times or curves") and §10's editor-convention note on the
per-axis checkbox both cover it — which per [`PILOT.md`](../../PILOT.md) is
exactly the kind worth recording.

Also judge-side, invisible to every figure above: the pilot draws the two slots
in the **opposite array order** to the reference (`slots.order` 1/2 with both
names matched, so here the measure reads the swap itself, not a naming gap).
Both baselines made the same swap. Three agents independently choosing the same
"wrong" z-order suggests the frames never show the two parts overlapping — an
unobservable, like rung 8's remainder — rather than three identical mistakes.

## The reading

**Structure is a wash; motion is roughly half.** Name-agnostically all three
runs author the identical skeleton. On motion the pilot lands at about **2× the
MAE** of the like-for-like baseline (attempt 2) on both sets, with `animations`
at 0.768 against 0.936, key economy at 0.167 against 0.710, and one timeline
kind short per moving bone. Its slot *placement* is as good as the baseline's
(worst drift 0.9 px vs 0.8 px) — per-frame pixel fitting does place sprites —
but everything the rung is named for, timing and spacing as *structure* (sparse
keys, curves, split axes), is where it fell short, and each shortfall is one
the guide already answers in §4.4, §8 and §10.

A weaker model on a stronger harness reached T2 clean in three hours for about
160k self-reported tokens, produced a valid, well-formed run record, and every
one of its shortfalls is legible in the instruments without reading a single
line of its prose. That is the pilot protocol doing what it was built for.
